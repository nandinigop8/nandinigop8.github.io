Nasal Cannula
Dislodgement Detector

Detecting Nasal Cannula Dislodgement in Low-Resource Settings

Team Project Pratt School of Engineering, Duke University Client: Mulago National Referral Hospital, Uganda
MOTIVATION & OVERVIEW

An early-warning system for a problem nurses can't watch for constantly.

A nasal cannula delivers supplemental oxygen through a flexible tube with two prongs that rest in the nostrils. Infants in respiratory distress rely on this for continuous oxygen support, but the tube can easily become dislodged as babies move.

At Mulago National Referral Hospital in Kampala, Uganda, nurse-to-baby ratios can reach 1:35, which makes constant manual checking for dislodgement impractical. Undetected dislodgement can interrupt oxygen delivery and lead to serious harm. Our team designed a system that alerts nurses the moment a cannula comes loose, so monitoring no longer depends on someone watching every infant at once.

SOLUTION

Three components working together.

A self-securing silicone mold fits over the cannula prongs with an embedded thermistor that monitors skin temperature. If readings stay below a calibrated threshold for ten consecutive counts, the alarm activates. A 3D-printed alarm box houses the circuitry, battery, and alarm, and can be opened easily to swap the battery.

We also added an LCD screen that displays the live thermistor reading and prints a clear "DISLODGED!" message the moment the alarm triggers, alongside three status LEDs: green while powered, yellow while calibrating, and red when the alarm sounds.

COMPONENT BREAKDOWN

Mold Attachment

Silicone mold with embedded thermistor, fitted over the cannula prongs.

Alarm Box

Circuitry & Display

Rapidity of Alert Alerts within 1–3 minutes Time from dislodgement to alarm activation PASS — avg. 17.29s across 20 trials
Accuracy 95% accurate True/false positive and negative tracking across hold times PASS — all true positives (60 trials) and true negatives (10 trials)
Safety Skin-friendly, no electrical threats Heat, connection, and voltage check against IEC 60601 TBD — needs professional testing for full IEC 60601 compliance
Reusability ~1000 uses Disassemble, clean, and inspect for wear PASS — no wear after 50 cycles; unaffected by hand sanitizer

CONCLUSION & FUTURE WORK

The final device reliably alerts nurses when an infant's nasal cannula becomes dislodged, using a thin, flexible silicone mold with an embedded thermistor to monitor temperature at the nose.

Since presenting at the Pratt Design Expo, we've already added the LCD readout shown above. Next, we'd like to further tune the alarm's frequency and pitch for a hospital setting, and pursue formal IEC 60601 safety testing.
